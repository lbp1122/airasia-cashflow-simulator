Model methodology and limitations

Actual reported base figures — Q2 2026

Quarterly revenue RM5,085.584M; fuel expense RM2,796.020M at reported average jet fuel price US$183/bbl; staff RM563.000M; aircraft maintenance and overhaul RM537.342M; airport/user charges RM506.509M; operating lease expense RM19.873M; other expenses RM269.503M; other income RM49.252M. These reproduce the reported EBITDA of RM442.589M.

Source: AirAsia Group Q2 2026 interim financial statements and associated Q2 results.

Which costs automatically change with revenue?
  • Fuel: 100% of consumption proportional to the revenue/volume proxy; barrel price applied independently.
  • Airport/user charges: 100% variable.
  • Maintenance expense: 30% variable, 70% fixed.
  • Staff expense: 20% variable, 80% fixed.
  • Other operating expenses: 40% variable, 60% fixed.
  • Operating lease expense and other income: fixed.

These behavior percentages are analyst assumptions, not company disclosures. A lower ticket price could reduce revenue without reducing flight activity or fuel burn.

Cash financing costs and investment allowance

Quarterly proxy = one half of 1H 2026 reported cash items: interest RM254.894M / 2 = RM127.447M; lease liability repayments RM1,207.371M / 2 = RM603.686M; bank/debenture principal repayments (RM1,050.152M + RM147.170M) / 2 = RM598.661M; income taxes RM20.558M / 2 = RM10.279M. Sustaining capex is an assumed RM150M per quarter.

Historical financing repayments are not a debt-maturity schedule. The separate refinancing comparison removes the modeled principal repayment but retains interest, leases and capex. It assumes equivalent refinancing cash is available, not loan forgiveness.

Break-even formulas

Volume factor = Selected revenue / RM5.085584B.

Fuel expense (RM B) = 2.796020 × (fuel price / 183) × volume factor.

Non-fuel operating expenses are calculated using the fixed/variable percentages above. EBITDA = revenue − fuel − non-fuel opex + other income.

Modeled net cash = EBITDA − cash interest − lease liability repayments − bank/debenture principal repayments − taxes − assumed capex.

Break-even fuel price solves modeled net cash = 0 at selected revenue. Minimum break-even revenue solves the same equation at selected barrel price, assuming revenue change corresponds to proportional flight activity. If contribution per extra RM of revenue is non-positive, revenue growth cannot create break-even within this simplified volume model.

Not covered — material risk of overstating cash flow
  • Extraordinary settlement of existing aircraft maintenance provisions or overdue payables.
  • Large aircraft deposits, purchase commitments, fleet additions and capex beyond RM150M per quarter.
  • Working-capital changes (advance ticket sales, supplier payments and collections).
  • Changing ticket yield, cabin occupancy, route mix, fuel efficiency or fleet composition.
  • Jet fuel hedges, realized fuel prices, currency fluctuations and financing-rate changes.
  • Future debt maturities, creditor concessions and new share issuance.

The output is a scenario sensitivity, not reported Q2 free cash flow, verified solvency, an investment recommendation or a forecast.
